Electromagnetic Levatation of a Magnet with Coil Overview

Inspired by my physics 2 class, I created an electromagnetic coil set up to levatate a small magnet. This design is inpspired by How To Do's design "Electromagnetic Levitation Device" on Instructables but with a few edits. The setup consists of an electromagnetic coil driven by a MOSFET, with feedback provided by a Hall effect sensor. The Hall sensor measures the perpendicular magnetic field generated by the levitating magnet. As the magnet approaches the coil, the magnetic field strength increases and is detected by the sensor. This feedback signal is used to control the MOSFET gate, which regulates current through the coil. When the magnet gets too close, the coil is switched off to reduce the magnetic pull. When the magnet begins to drop, the coil is switched back on to restore levitation. In paralell to the coil, an LED is attatched to monitor the off and on state of the coil.

As you can see, this attempt had a lot of things going right for it. The circuit seems to be operated as designed, but the coil looks to be too weak and unstable. My first idea to solve this was to try to increase to power supplied to the coil, but there were a couple of issues with that idea. When I made the coil, I had used a 3D printer to create the frame which means that the frame is very sensitive to heat. This combined with the fact that I had also used 28 AWG for the wire which ended up being too small as the coil would heat up when supplying anything above 3V and started melting the frame at low voltages. Increasing the voltage to the coil did not seem feasible. After trying smaller magnets, switching MOSFETs, even holding the coil together with small rubber bands, it did not seem like this coil could handle higher voltage, so the only option left was to remake the coil with a thicker base and a larger gauge wire.
